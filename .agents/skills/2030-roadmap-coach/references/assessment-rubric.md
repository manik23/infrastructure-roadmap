# Evidence-Based Learning Rubric

Use qualitative statuses and keep evidence alongside each status.

## Status definitions

- **Demonstrated:** The learner can explain the concept, apply it to an example, handle a relevant edge case and show evidence (tests, experiment, profile, model, design or operational drill).
- **Practiced:** A lab or implementation exists, but explanation, breadth of edge cases or verification is incomplete.
- **Review needed:** Core assumptions or concepts are misunderstood, or practical evidence reveals important gaps.
- **Not assessed:** No reliable evidence is available, or prerequisites/environment blocked the check.

## Review dimensions

1. Conceptual model and vocabulary
2. Assumptions, invariants and failure model
3. Implementation/design quality
4. Testing and verification
5. Measurement/observability
6. Trade-offs and limitations
7. Ability to communicate the result clearly

Not every dimension applies to every topic. Do not grade by lines of code, number of tools used or the amount of reading completed.

## End-of-phase review

For each phase:
- map the roadmap exit criteria to concrete evidence;
- list criteria met, partially met and not met;
- identify the highest-value gaps (maximum three remediation priorities at once);
- choose either advance, advance with a named refresher, or extend the phase;
- write a one-page retrospective: what improved, what remains weak, what to change in the next phase.

A failed experiment can be strong evidence of learning if the learner predicted a behavior, observed a different result, explains why and fixes the underlying issue. Hiding failed experiments is counterproductive.
